About 50% of the oxygen produced on Earth comes from the ocean, according to NOAA. That refers to ongoing oxygen production—not half of all the oxygen currently stored in the atmosphere. [oceanservice.noaa](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ocean-oxygen.html)

What produces it?

Most ocean oxygen comes from phytoplankton—tiny drifting algae and photosynthetic bacteria. They use sunlight to turn carbon dioxide and water into organic matter, releasing oxygen through photosynthesis. Seaweed also contributes, but microscopic organisms do most of the work.

An important distinction

The ocean also consumes roughly as much oxygen as it produces: marine organisms use it for respiration, including when microbes break down dead organic matter. So the ocean’s net addition of oxygen is much smaller than its total production. [oceanservice.noaa](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ocean-oxygen.html)

That’s why “every second breath comes from the ocean” is a memorable but oversimplified statement. The oxygen we breathe is drawn from an atmospheric reservoir accumulated over hundreds of millions of years—not simply from today’s ocean photosynthesis. The most accurate short answer is: the ocean accounts for roughly half of Earth’s current oxygen production.
