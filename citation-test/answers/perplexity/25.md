Usually, pay off high-interest debt first, but don’t assume you need to be debt-free before investing. A common approach is to capture your employer’s retirement match, maintain emergency savings, and prioritize expensive debt while continuing scheduled payments on cheaper loans.

How to decide

The key comparison is your debt’s interest cost versus your investment’s potential return—after taxes and with risk taken into account. Paying down debt delivers predictable interest savings; investment returns are uncertain. An investment with an expected return equal to your debt rate isn’t an equally safe alternative.

Why the interest rate matters so much

Paying extra toward a 20% credit card balance avoids interest at a rate that is difficult to reliably beat through investing. With a 3% fixed-rate mortgage, the hurdle is much lower, so accepting investment risk may be worthwhile over a long horizon. Neither comparison means stock-market returns are guaranteed.

It also doesn’t have to be all-or-nothing. Splitting extra money between moderate-rate debt and retirement investing can balance interest savings, long-term growth, and the peace of mind of owing less.
