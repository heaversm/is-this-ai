Starting 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act required greater transparency about when people are interacting with AI and when certain content is AI-generated or manipulated. These duties come mainly from Article 50. EU-level and national enforcement also began on that date.

What the requirements covered
- Chatbots and other interactive AI: Providers must tell people they are interacting with AI rather than a human, unless that is already obvious from the circumstances. [morganlewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)
- Machine-readable content marking: Providers of systems that generate synthetic text, images, audio or video must make their outputs detectable as artificially generated or manipulated—for example, through machine-readable markings. This is separate from a visible label that a reader or viewer sees. [morganlewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)
- Deepfake disclosures: Organizations using AI to generate or manipulate deepfake images, audio or video must disclose their artificial origin. [artificialintelligenceact](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- Public-interest text: Those publishing AI-generated text to inform the public about matters of public interest must disclose that it is AI-generated, unless it has undergone human review or editorial control and a person or organization takes editorial responsibility. [artificialintelligenceact](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- Emotion recognition and biometric categorization: Organizations deploying these systems must inform the people exposed to them. [morganlewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)

Important timing distinction

The August deadline did not mean every AI-generated item needed a visible “Made with AI” label. The law distinguishes between providers’ technical marking duties and users’ or publishers’ disclosure duties for particular uses, such as deepfakes and public-interest text. [morganlewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)

There was also a limited transition: generative AI systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 had until 2 December 2026 to meet the machine-readable marking requirement. That extension did not delay the chatbot, deepfake, public-interest-text or biometric disclosure duties. [morganlewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)
