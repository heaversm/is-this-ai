No—knuckle cracking has not been shown to cause arthritis. Studies comparing habitual knuckle-crackers with people who don’t crack their knuckles have not found an increased rate of hand arthritis.

Is it otherwise harmful?

Gentle, painless cracking is generally not a concern. The important distinction is between a harmless popping sound and forcing a joint: forceful manipulation can cause injury, and cracking your knuckles shouldn’t hurt. [clevelandclinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/is-cracking-your-knuckles-bad-for-you)

Some older research linked habitual cracking with hand swelling or reduced grip strength, but that doesn’t establish that cracking caused those problems—or that it causes arthritis. [arthritis-uk](https://www.arthritis-uk.org/news/does-cracking-your-knuckles-cause-arthritis/)

If cracking comes with persistent pain, swelling, or stiffness, stop forcing the joint and have it checked rather than assuming it’s just the habit.
