A typical plastic water or soda bottle is commonly estimated to take about 450 years to break down. That’s a rough estimate, not a precise countdown for every bottle.

The important distinction is that “breaking down” doesn’t necessarily mean disappearing. Sunlight, wind, and water gradually fragment plastic into smaller pieces, including microplastics, which can remain in the environment long after the bottle loses its shape. [repository.library.noaa](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/38554/noaa_38554_DS1.pdf)

The actual rate depends on the type of plastic and environmental conditions, including exposure to sunlight and weather. So the practical takeaway is “centuries of persistence,” rather than “completely gone after 450 years.”
