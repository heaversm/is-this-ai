Generally, no—unless the package explicitly says it’s safe to eat raw. Ordinary homemade cookie dough and store-bought dough intended for baking can contain harmful bacteria. Even a taste can expose you to food poisoning. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/no-raw-dough.html)

Why raw dough is risky

There are two separate concerns:
- Raw flour can contain bacteria such as E. coli. Flour isn’t automatically safe to eat just because it’s dry or processed.
- Raw eggs can contain Salmonella. Removing the eggs—or using pasteurized eggs—doesn’t address the risk from untreated flour.

Baking the dough according to the recipe or package directions kills these germs. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/no-raw-dough.html)

What you can eat instead

Choose cookie dough explicitly labeled “edible” or “safe to eat raw,” and follow its storage instructions. These products use heat-treated flour and pasteurized eggs, or no eggs. The dough in commercially made cookie-dough ice cream is also treated to make it safe to eat without baking.

The practical rule: check the label rather than assuming all refrigerated dough—or all egg-free dough—is safe. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/no-raw-dough.html)
