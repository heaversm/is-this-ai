Usually not—especially for electronics and appliances. Repairs during the coverage period are relatively uncommon, and the plan can cost as much as the repair; you’re often better off setting the money aside.

It may make sense if a covered failure could cause a major bill you couldn’t comfortably pay, or for a car you’ll keep beyond its factory warranty—but check exclusions, deductibles, and the total cost first. [cnbc](https://www.cnbc.com/select/is-an-extended-car-warranty-worth-it/)
