Yes—nuclear power is substantially safer than coal per unit of electricity generated, when safety is measured by deaths from air pollution and accidents. Our World in Data’s comparison estimates about 99.8% fewer deaths for nuclear than coal. [ourworldindata](https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy)

How large is the difference?

A common measure is deaths per terawatt-hour (TWh), where one TWh equals one billion kilowatt-hours of electricity:Electricity sourceEstimated deaths per TWhCoalAbout 25NuclearAbout 0.03

These estimates imply roughly 800 times fewer deaths for nuclear for the same electricity output. They are population-level averages—not predictions that a particular plant will cause that number of deaths. [ourworldindata](https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy)

Why coal is more dangerous

The biggest difference is routine air pollution, not spectacular accidents. Most of coal’s estimated deaths come from pollution during normal operation; mining and supply-chain accidents add to its toll. Nuclear plants do not burn fuel, so they avoid the combustion-related air pollution produced by coal plants.

The nuclear estimate also does not simply ignore major disasters: it includes deaths attributed to Chernobyl and Fukushima, including indirect deaths associated with Fukushima’s evacuation rather than only radiation fatalities. Even with those included, nuclear’s estimated mortality per unit of electricity remains far below coal’s. [ourworldindata](https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy)

What the numbers don’t settle

The exact ratio is uncertain. Estimates depend on pollution controls, population exposure, how accident-related deaths are attributed, and assumptions about long-term health effects. The comparison also does not fully capture every supply-chain health impact or every possible future risk. Our World in Data notes that its coal estimate is based on older European research and may understate the global burden. [ourworldindata](https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy)

So “safer” does not mean risk-free, nor does a mortality comparison settle every question about nuclear power. But on the specific question of deaths per unit of electricity, the evidence strongly favors nuclear over coal—and that comparison excludes the additional long-term harms of climate change.
