For most healthy adults, up to 400 milligrams (mg) of caffeine per day is generally considered safe. Treat that as an upper limit—not a goal—because some people experience side effects at much lower amounts. [fda](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)

What that looks like in drinks

That’s roughly four 8-ounce cups of brewed coffee, or two to three 12-ounce cups, depending on strength. Coffee’s caffeine content varies widely, so check the specific drink rather than relying on the number of “cups.” Count caffeine from all sources, including tea, soda, energy drinks, supplements, chocolate, and some medicines.

When to aim lower
- Pregnancy: Keep intake below 200 mg per day, according to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists’ guidance. [ama-assn](https://www.ama-assn.org/public-health/prevention-wellness/what-doctors-want-patients-know-about-impact-caffeine)
- Sensitivity or symptoms: Cut back if you notice insomnia, anxiety, jitters, a racing heart, or palpitations—even if you’re under 400 mg. Your personal limit may be lower.
- Medical conditions or medications: Ask your clinician or pharmacist whether these change your caffeine limit. If you’re trying to become pregnant or breastfeeding, discuss an appropriate limit with your clinician as well. [fda](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)
- Children and teens: The adult 400 mg guideline doesn’t apply; caffeine is not recommended for children. [mayoclinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/in-depth/caffeine/art-20045678)

Avoid pure or highly concentrated caffeine powders and liquids: small measurement errors can deliver dangerous doses.
