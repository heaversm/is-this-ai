No—for most people, taking vitamin C supplements does not prevent colds. Taking it regularly before you get sick may slightly shorten a cold, but starting it after symptoms appear has not shown a consistent benefit.

What the research shows
- Prevention: A Cochrane review covering 29 trial comparisons and 11,306 participants found that regular supplementation did not reduce cold incidence in the general population. [cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000980_vitamin-c-preventing-and-treating-common-cold)
- Duration: People who took vitamin C regularly had colds that were about 8% shorter in adults and 14% shorter in children. For an adult with a weeklong cold, that amounts to roughly half a day—not a dramatic difference. Symptoms may also be somewhat milder. [cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000980_vitamin-c-preventing-and-treating-common-cold)
- An exception: Vitamin C reduced cold risk in small studies of people undergoing brief periods of extreme physical stress, such as marathon runners, skiers, and soldiers training in subarctic conditions. That finding does not necessarily apply to everyday exercise or ordinary winter weather.
- After symptoms start: Trials have not found a consistent reduction in cold duration or severity when supplements are started only once someone becomes ill. [cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000980_vitamin-c-preventing-and-treating-common-cold)

What that means for you

Routine vitamin C supplements aren’t justified solely to prevent colds in the average person. The distinction is between getting enough of an essential nutrient and taking extra: vitamin C supports normal immune function, but extra doses haven’t been shown to keep most people from catching a cold.
