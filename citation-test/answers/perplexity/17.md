No—the three-second memory is a myth. Goldfish can retain learned information for weeks or months, not just a few seconds. [livescience](https://www.livescience.com/goldfish-memory.html)

Researchers have demonstrated this through training experiments. At Plymouth University, goldfish learned to press a lever to receive food. When food was available only during a particular hour each day, they also learned when to press it—showing that they could remember both an action and its timing. [discoverwildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/fish/goldfish-memory)

There isn’t one fixed “memory length” for goldfish: how long they remember depends on the task and the experience. They’ve also learned to navigate mazes and escape nets, and remembered those tasks weeks or months later. So their memories don’t simply reset every three seconds.
