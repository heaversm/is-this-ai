Teenagers ages 13–18 need 8–10 hours of sleep each night, on a regular basis, according to the American Academy of Sleep Medicine’s recommendation cited by the CDC. Around 9 hours is a useful target.

Putting that into practice
- If a teen wakes at 7 a.m., being asleep by 10 p.m. allows for nine hours of sleep—so bedtime should leave some extra time to fall asleep.
- Keep sleep and wake times consistent rather than relying on weekend catch-up sleep; getting enough sleep regularly is more beneficial. [health.clevelandclinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-much-sleep-teens-need)
- Puberty naturally shifts the body clock later, making it harder for teens to fall asleep early—even when they still need to get up early for school. [health.clevelandclinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-much-sleep-teens-need)

If a teen regularly feels very sleepy despite getting the recommended amount, talk with their healthcare provider. [nhlbi.nih](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/how-much-sleep)
