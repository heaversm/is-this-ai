No—you cannot get influenza from a flu vaccine, whether it’s a shot or the nasal spray. Flu shots contain killed virus or viral proteins, neither of which can cause infection. The nasal spray contains weakened viruses that also cannot cause flu illness. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/vaccines/keyfacts.html)

Why you might feel sick afterward
- Vaccine side effects can feel a little like flu. A sore arm, mild fever, headache, tiredness, or muscle aches can follow vaccination. These reflect your immune system’s response, not influenza infection, and generally resolve within a day or two.
- You may have caught flu around the same time. Protection takes about two weeks to develop, so an infection acquired shortly before or after vaccination can still make you sick. The timing can make it seem as though the vaccine caused it. [clevelandclinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/can-the-flu-shot-give-you-the-flu)
- You can still catch flu despite vaccination. Protection isn’t perfect, but vaccination can reduce how severe the illness becomes if you do get infected. That’s getting flu despite the vaccine—not from it. [cdc](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/vaccines/keyfacts.html)
